Health & Safety

Last updated: 14 August 2026 · Applies to Lumba for iPhone

Lumba is not a medical device. It gives general exercise guidance. It does not diagnose anything and it does not replace a doctor or a physiotherapist. Stop exercising and seek medical advice if you feel sharp pain, numbness, tingling, weakness, dizziness, or if your symptoms get worse.

Who should check with a professional first

Please get clearance from a healthcare professional before starting the programme if you:

  • are pregnant, or have given birth in the last six months;
  • have had surgery in the last six months;
  • have osteoporosis, a diagnosed spinal condition, or a disc injury;
  • have numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm or leg;
  • have pain that wakes you at night, or that followed a fall or accident;
  • have any change in bladder or bowel control.

The app asks about these during setup. If you tell it one applies to you, it will say so — please take that seriously rather than skipping past it.

Exercising safely

  • Work within a pain-free range. "No pain, no gain" does not apply to back rehabilitation.
  • Use the Too hard control during a session — the plan adjusts rather than pushing you.
  • Move slowly and breathe. If you are holding your breath, the movement is too hard.
  • Lumba cannot see you. It cannot tell whether your form is correct or whether an exercise suits your condition.
  • Mild muscle fatigue the next day is normal. Sharp, radiating or worsening pain is not — stop and get it looked at.

How the posture score is calculated

You take one side-profile photo. Apple's Vision framework, running entirely on your iPhone, locates body landmarks and the app measures four values from them. Each is scored out of 100 and the four are averaged into your total.

The scan detail screen inside the app shows you each measured value next to its target, so the number is never a black box.

What the score is and is not

It is an in-app indicator for comparing your own scans over time. It is affected by your clothing, the camera angle, the lighting and how you happened to stand in that moment.

It is not a clinical assessment, not a diagnosis, and not comparable to another person's score. A low score does not mean something is wrong with you, and a high score does not mean you have no risk of back pain.
